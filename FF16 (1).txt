Folio One: Death Bells
Ding, ding, ding, ding. The echoing of the bell at the entrance of the graveyard seems to make the ground beneath it tremble. A pale and somewhat jaundiced arm rings that rusted bell again and again. The clapper swings with every shake.
A pair of mourners is led past by a weeping mother - an old and portly lady who can barely walk. The trembling hand rings the bell again, this time with more vigor and less patience.
The mourners maintain a distance of at least six feet away from each other - the guidelines from the Imperial Ministry of Health (IMOH) are quite insistent about how far to stay away from one another.
The ringer of the bell wildly cries out, "Gone! Gone! Woe has befallen the Empire! Woe has befallen the Colonies! Blessed is the Emperor! Blessed is Empire!"
The members of the Orange Guard take no chances. They, too, maintain their distance whilst escorting these mourners through the entrance to the graveyard. Their orange uniforms lack any of the luster that once graced them and are now faded and dirty.
Some wear facemasks whilst others have no other choice but to wrap thick pieces of cloth around their mouths and noses.
A member of the Guard has his bolt-action rifle slung across his back. A hand rests on the pommel of his sword as he and his companion usher the mourners past the wrought-iron gate.
There's a slight twitch to the guard's eyes as he turns his head slightly to catch a glimpse of the weeping mother.
The old woman's eyes are hollow. Her steps are heavy and labored. Her movements are sluggish and slow. She has neither said a single word nor shed a single tear since arriving here at the graveyard.
The pallbearers are dressed in the finest of the Empire's garments - black coats, white collars, and purple cravats. The clapper of the bell rings out again and again.
"Woe hath befallen us! Woe hath befallen us!"
A pallbearer who looks no more than eighteen years of age scoffs, "Get that blasted mendicant out of here! He's disrupting things!"
The woman behind him, hushes him, "That 'mendicant' as you so rudely remark is your uncle...or what's left of him, anyway."
"He's annoying and distracting everyone."
"Leave. Him."
The boy forces a sigh through his teeth, "Fine." When he breathes out, his glasses fog up and he blinks a few times as it slowly clears away.
The mendicant in question is not wearing any kind of protection on his pock-marked face - he perhaps believes himself invincible or lucky to have survived this long. His cracked lips are moving and he's uttering something or another, but no words are discernible from the mumble.
All he wears is a simple sackcloth. His hair is wild and unkempt. His limbs are frail and thin. A far cry from the man who used to be the treasurer of this seaside town.
There are other mourners who stand at the graveside, some of them are family members and the rest are simply members of the community.
The palls are heavier than they look. Demand soared and the prices for just one of them had risen so high, that the makers of these palls had made a sizable fortune from the sale of just one alone.
In the palls, many bodies are wrapped up in fine silks. It was either that or letting the Orange Guard burn the bodies with the flamethrowers - chances cannot be taken when it comes to such weighty matters.
The stench of death is the only thing that fills the air. Even now, other members of the Guard stand as though they are ready to perform an execution via firing. They raise their flamethrowers high in the air, steady themselves, and wait for the signal from their Commander.
The Commander in question somberly says, "May Emperor forgive us. Burn the bodies!"
A pile of bodies are then set aflame. Some of the passing mourners gasp or avert their gaze. The dead corpses will take time to burn - the stench just got a whole lot worse.
The mendicant rings the bell again and again, his movements becoming more and more frantic and his arms and hands trembling with every swing.
"Woe are the Colonies! Woe is to the Empire! Death has befallen us all! Blessed is the Emperor!"
Someone asks the closest member of the Guard, "Shoot him already."
The member of the Guard adjusts her mask and shakes her head from side to side, "I'm afraid that I--I cannot do that."
The mourner rudely asks, "Why not?"
"He has already been infected - numerous times. And each time, by some or the other miracle which I lack the education to explain, he has somehow survived. Perhaps he's the Emperor's will, or the Emperor's blessing, hmm?"
"Uncle Thomas has been driven to madness. He is not a miracle; he is a statistical abnormality. And he's not even wearing a mask. Stupid man."
The member of the Orange Guard holds onto her bolt action rifle just a little tighter than before but does not lift it from its place.
The palls are finally set down at their intended grave sites. The mendicant keeps ringing the bell and saying those same words again and again. But by now, the others tune his ramblings out.
A small child of not quite seven-years-old is standing amongst the others who are at the graveside. He, too, is wearing his mask as mandated by the IMOH guidelines - even children are not immune. This was a lesson that was, unfortunately, learned the hard way.
The boy looks at the body in the pall with some measure of sadness and wonderment. He keeps fiddling with his heavy gloves.
His mother has her hands on the boy's shoulders as she quietly observes. His father maintains a ramrod posture and his lips are pursed into a straight line. He is clad in proper mourning attire. His hazel eyes are locked onto the horizon and seem to have no intention of looking at anything else.
Smoke billows in the farthest corner of the horizon. Black clouds hang above the graveyard. Small squads of the Orange Guard patrol the area.
The priest was one of the first to succumb to the horrors, so the next best thing was a filing clerk. He is in his forties, clean-shaven and quite nervous. He reads off a few points which he has hastily scribbled onto a piece of flimsy paper, "We are gathered here today to honor the memory of--of the members of the Raffles family."
The filing clerk pauses before continuing, "To lose the last of one's entire family in a single stroke is--is nothing short of brutal."
A murmur of assent goes around.
The clerk gulps and proceeds, "It has only--only been a few months since this senseless tragedy befell our quiet town. We thought ourselves invincible, untouchable. We believed that it would only affect...other people. Those kinds of people. N--not us." The manner in which he says this indicates a certain prejudice.
Nevertheless, he painfully admits, "But no, it affects us all. It discriminates not by origin, birth, sex, age, creed, or--or whatever else. We are all vulnerable to it - a lesson that was taught to us harshly and without any mercy. But it is in the grace of Emperor that we are still here today."
A higher-ranking member of the Guard nudges the clerk and harshly whispers something in his ear. The clerk feebly nods and looks around.
He wraps it up by saying, "We--we mustn't spend any more time outside than is absolutely necessary. The town's interim council has decided to implement the lockdown...starting in a few hours' time. A quick word of prayer before we send these souls off to their last resting place."
Even the members of the Orange Guard bow their heads in prayer. Not like there's much point in it, but what else is one supposed to do in a situation like this?
Thomas sits on the ground and rocks back and forth. He babbles about random things that don't quite make any sense.
"I have seen wondrous visions! I have beholden wondrous things!"
A member of the guard melees him with the butt of his rifle. Thomas falls to the ground and groans in pain.
"SILENCE! Have you no respect for the dead?!"
Thomas clutches the wound on his head and crawls away. The brief prayer comes to its conclusion, and the mourners of the dead begin their solemn journey back to their homes. On the way back, some stop by the shops. The lines are quite long.
As of late, a system of rationing has been implemented. Every day, the food stores are counted and recorded. They don't want to admit it, but things aren't looking too good. As such, extra regiments of the Orange Guard have been dispatched and are stationed at every corner of the town.
At least some are able to afford a funeral and are able to bury their loved ones. Many others have no such privilege and are forced to watch on in horror as the bodies are incinerated with the help of the flamethrowers.
A lady puts a few cans of peas inside her purse. She drops a few coins on a sterile tin plate. Skin-to-skin contact should be kept to a minimum. Yet another IMOH guideline that came far too late.
The woman is gaunt and the skin on her face hangs loosely. Her husband is a doctor who was called to the frontlines in this battle. She has not received any mail from him in weeks. She fears that the worst may have already happened. For all intents and purposes, she is now the sole breadwinner for her children.
The lines of unfortunate souls have not shrunk since the early hours of the morning. The Guard keeps a close eye on everyone. Their faces are covered and their lips are set in a straight line.
One of them warns someone, "Six feet apart!"
That person raises both hands in the air and takes a few steps back. A small child's voice can be heard asking his mother, "Why do we have to stay away from each other?"
The parent gently answers, "Because that is the rule. Stay away from others until further notice."
Thomas sits at the edge of a bench, his knees are drawn up to his chest, and his grimy hands are on his cheeks.
"'Twas a dream...'twas a dream. Something great and might sweeping across the lands of--of Empire...Colony and Core alike. Tremble. Tremble, ye mighty ones!"
After the funeral...
Large gatherings are expressly forbidden. Yet another lesson learned far too late. As such, only a few people sit around a large supper table and eat whatever gruel passes for food during times such as these.
Thomas sits at the end of the table, his eyes are bloodshot, and his lips are bloody. He was invited more out of pity, but everyone does their best to maintain their distance from him.
He says, "Blessed is Emperor for--for the sustenance that graces our tables." The frail man brings the bowl up to his lips and gulps the contents therein.
Annabelle grimaces at how messy he's being. She averts her gaze and pokes the mashed potatoes and carrots scattered on her chipped porcelain plate. She scoops up some of it with her fork and brings it up to her mouth.
"This is not a feast fit for people like us," she says while covering her mouth and not making eye contact with anyone.
She forces herself to swallow the food before going on, "Where is the meat? Some pheasant or--or beef, perhaps? I heard that the Edgeworths are doing rather well with their hog farms."
Percy wipes his mouth with a napkin and informs her, "I heard that the Orange Guard have been taking them."
"They have?"
"Oh, yes. Soldiers, above all, need their meat more than the rest of us, it would seem. Other livestock are taken and executed because there's a chance that they, too, might be infected."
Thomas nods, "Y--yes. Precautionary measures. W--wise. I don't remember what...meat tastes like. Even smells elude me now. I--I used to have so much meat. The--the old wife always complained about--"
He breaks down into tears and softly sobs when remembering his wife. Even a small child starts to cry and Annabelle picks her up. She rocks the child up and down, hastily making her exit from the dining room.
In between sobs, Thomas asks, "What happened to us? How did it become so--so bad? So blighted? We thought that it wouldn't affect us."
Percy lowers his head and notes, "We were naive. Unprepared. And if I had any strength left in my body, I'd strangle you. The town treasurer...how could you not know, not care?!"
Thomas wipes his tears away, "We were all caught off guard by it! The initial reports CLEARLY mentioned how this had only been affecting the Colonies of Empire! The Core was absolutely safe. I--I thought it was some kind of an endemic thing amongst the savages and barbarians of the Colonies. That's what EVERY telegram and radio report claimed!"
Percy points his spoon at Thomas, "Well, it wasn't just something that the savages caught, was it? Did they spread it? Did they bring it to our venerable shores?"
Thomas shakes his head, "No. That...I--I don't know. I don't know what's true anymore; I have no idea what's going on, let alone what the Empire's plans are on--on dealing with this."
Barnaby picks something out of his teeth and lets out a small belch, "The Core is all that matters. To hell with the Colonies. I regularly speak on the telephone with someone high up in the ranks of the Guard, and they claim that battleplans are being drawn up for a full-scale invasion of the Colonies."
He wipes his finger on the plate and proceeds, "If they won't give us what we're owed, then we're just about ready to take it by force. Besides, many of the Colonies have been trying to rise up against our...benevolent governance. So, we've been given a fair pretext for what is otherwise a justified, ahem, response."
Percy clicks his tongue, "Crushing the Colonies and ransacking them - while not a bad idea on paper - isn't exactly something that'll be sustainable for the long run, old boy. We need our Emperor now more than ever. We need His wisdom, His guidance."
Barnaby mirthlessly chuckles, "Yes, maybe we do, but there's still our resource shortages and the looming threat of everything else."
Thomas drinks some water and lets out a contented sigh, "We--we have to prepare ourselves. It has only been mere months since these horrors have paid us a visit, but I feel like years have already passed by. The fear and anxiety of not knowing whether or not I am infected - or whole - every passing day is taking a toll on me."
Barnaby remarks, "You're a lucky bastard, you. If only Lady Luck had us all under her watchful gaze."
Percy waves a hand dismissively, "Luck has very little to do with it. It is simple statistics, old boy. Genetics, environment, diet, exposure - it's all quite simple. I wouldn't wish to bog any of you down with the details - we hear enough of that on the IMOH broadcasts. Suffice to say that the odds are stacked against us."
Fearful gasps and whispers can be heard throughout the dinner table.
Annabelle returns and asks Thomas to leave. She tries to do it in a way that doesn't come off as too rude. She tells him that the children in the house are rather afraid of his entire demeanor.
The former treasurer pensively lowers his head and leaves. The others give him a wide berth as he trudges out.
Annabelle looks at him with sympathy, "I'm sorry, but we--we simply cannot take any chances. Please, try and understand. I can call the Abbey and ask if they--"
Thomas is unable to look her in the eye. He slowly shakes his head, "It is no worry, my dear. What has befallen me has befallen me. If I must die tomorrow, then it is as Emperor wills it. If I must live, then it is as Emperor wills it, too. Regardless, I shall find my way. Please, say hello to the children for me. Do you still have that carved toy?"
Annabelle forces a smile and sniffles, "Yes. I--I do."
Thomas smiles back, "My memory is not what it was, my dear. Was it an elephant or a bear?"
"An elephant. You said that a subject from one of the Colonies - a child - taught you how to carve it."
"Hmm, yes. I shan't be taking any more of your time, my dear. You have children to bathe and--and put to bed. May the Emperor watch over us all. I shall roam the streets."
She sighs, "Please, don't go too far--"
He breaks out into song and slowly sings as he weakly hobbles down the stairs. The housemaids immediately get to work. They don their protective masks and gloves and begin to wipe down the seat that Thomas had sat upon.
Outside, the sun is just setting. The darkening skies are stained a reddish color. A few gunshots ring out in the distance.
The streets are mostly empty. Only sparse patrols of the Orange Guard roam the streets. There are many houses which are boarded up and shuttered. Many businesses are either condemned, quarantined indefinitely, or are just simply empty.
The stench of death doesn't abate. If anything, it's getting stronger and stronger. An Orange Guard patrol simply passes by Thomas, who is now sitting on a bench overlooking the ocean.
He mumbles, "Death awaits us all. Death has come upon us. 'Tis only a--a matter of time till we are all swept up by it."
The wind is strong, carrying with it odors foul and unmentionable. Thomas has already lost his sense of smell - one of the initial signs of being infected.
He leans forward and clasps his hands together, "May the Emperor protect us all. May the regions and territories of the Imperial Core be kept safe from the blighting ravages."
He slowly closes his eyes and starts praying. After doing that, he rises up and politely waves hello to two small children who shouldn't be outside past the set curfew time. They keep their distance and hastily hurry along the cobblestoned street.
The former treasurer then sits back down on the bench and watches a few members of the Guard unloading bodies onto the sandy shores of the beach.
A single member of the Orange Guard walks over and pokes him with his rifle, "You. Get up. We need help. Any help, at this point."
Thomas keeps his hands raised, so as to indicate that he is unarmed - the Guard is just waiting for a reason to shoot, and they'll seize upon any provocation.
The member of the Guard then turns around and jerks his chin at the shore below, "Help us tally the dead. I don't know how it's possible, but our new Regiment Commander is an imbecile. And I myself cannot read. We require somebody to help us count and mark them all. You are educated, are you not, my good sir?"
Thomas adjusts his rags and answers in the affirmative, "Yes, yes, very much so. I even went to college. I--I think. You will forgive me if I am slow. I am not as sharp as--as I once was. You see..."
The member of the Orange Guard interjects, "I don't care about any of that. Our orders are to burn the corpses soon after they are counted. If we're not quick, Emperor knows what will happen."
"Ah, yes. Fair enough. In my youth, I visited a Colony. The locals were rather accommodating, which came to me as a huge shock, really!"
Along the way to the beach, the mendicant goes on and on about his journeys through the width, length, and breadth of Empire.
On the way, the soldier is forced to listen and take note of the details, many of which go over his head. He stops a few times to make some adjustments to his metallic mask and to check the bullets in his rifle.
Down at the shore, some of the Guards are talking amongst themselves.
Beth straightens her back and prods, "Why not put those savages in the Colonies to work? Wouldn't that address our...err...resource shortages and what have you?"
Tom clicks his tongue and winces, "As it is, we're already reeling from the Great War, and those blasted uprisings have started popping up like weeds. Those barbarians - I am told - are already demanding for the right to elect their own representatives, can you believe it? From amidst their own kind. Is His Holiness not good enough for them?"
"Where is the Lord Emperor, anyway? I--I only saw Him once when I was but a little girl. His carriage passed through my village."
Tom shrugs elaborately, "I know not what the plans are for these matters, Beth. High Command only tells us what we must needs know. Everything else is someone else's problem. Hmph, look, that blasted bell-ringing mendicant is coming our way. Tch."
"You harbor dislike for the man. Why? What has he done to you?"
"I know not much of politics and such things. All I know is that they should've been better prepared for the tide. He was the treasurer! Where--where did all that coin go, pray tell?"
Beth shrugs back at him, "Into the sea, mayhaps?"
Thomas approaches the gathered Guards with clasped hands. His back is slightly hunched, and he seems to have trouble walking. He winces terribly when he sees all the wrapped-up bodies piled on the sand.
The man who escorted him here gives him a slight nudge, "We need help in counting this before we 'ave to light the fires. You can read, eh?"
Thomas incredulously asks the gathered Guards, "Can--can none of you read??"
Shaking heads all around.
The mendicant sits on a large rock and asks for a writing implement and a piece of paper. It takes a good five minutes for someone to return to him with the requested items.
Thomas furrows his brow and beckons for the Guards to lay the bodies out one by one. He goes over the body and writes down the details on a sheet of stained paper. Gunshots echo from the distance, but nobody pays much mind to it. They've become inured to this sort of thing by now.
By the time it's all done, Thomas announces, "Twenty-six souls. Poor, poor souls. Some of these shrouds are...rather tiny. I shan't venture a further guess. Personal effects?"
A Guard replies in the negative, "Not anymore. We had to firebomb them, sir. Given the severity of the situation in the other townships, an aerial bombardment was--was deemed necessary."
Thomas's face scrunches up. He looks away and mutters, "Blast! I don't see tags on any of them. Do you at least know their names? Did the doctors--"
The Guard gravely replies, "They ARE the doctors...and nurses, too. Or were. We found them dead in their makeshift hospital. Bodies strewn everywhere. Twenty-six of them, yes?"
Thomas covers his mouth with his hand, "Emperor have mercy. Y--yes, twenty-six of them. I need someone to sign here."
Since the members of the Guard can't even read, they mark the sheet of paper with a simple "X". With shaking hands, Thomas adds his signature to the bottom and hands it back to the Guard.
The man who brough him here says, "You're welcome to stay, but I recommend that you...err...take your leave, sir. Not everyone has the stomach for what's about to happen next. And the smells alone haunt you more than you know, sir."
Thomas agrees, "Yes. I shall take my leave now, young man. What happened to us, hmm? Our Empire? Our Empire, I ask you?!"
"I am but a humble member of the Orange Guard, sir. Such questions go over my limited understanding of things. Sorry."
Thomas shakes his head and shuffles back up, muttering to himself all the while, "How did we fall so terribly?"
He finds his bell from earlier and goes around ringing it while loudly announcing, "GRIM ARE THE DAYS AHEAD! GRIM ARE THE DAYS AHEAD!"
After he's "asked" to stop, he hobbles over to the Abbey but is turned away at the door due to them being over-capacity. Even his usual bench is occupied by a masked beggar snoring away.
The bells of the Abbey ring out again. Thomas frantically rings his little bell along with the large bells of the Abbey.
"GRIM ARE THE NIGHTS AHEAD! Grim are--"
A member of the Orange Guard comes running over. She swiftly whacks the racket-causing mendicant on the back of his head with the butt of her rifle. Another Guard arrives and drags him off.
Thomas is forced to lie on the ground of what now counts as holding area for people who may still be loitering outside after curfew.
He weakly comes around and mutters, "I--I shall be all right, don't you worry about me. Ow! My--my head. Great god, that stings! Ow!" He grimaces when touching the wound and pulls his fingers back, covered in a bit of blood. He wipes his fingers on the nearby wall.
After a few moments, the Guards who escorted him arrive with yet another prisoner. This bearded tramp is dressed in a tattered suit. He's not wearing any kind of protection.
When he requests a mask, the Guards instead deliver a swift kick to his gut and lock him up in the same makeshift cell as Thomas. The floor is covered in hay meant for livestock. The cracked walls are a depressing shade of yellow.
The tramp groans and coughs rather violently. The Guards don't bother with any kind of medical treatment.
Thomas crawls over to the tramp and pleads with the Orange Guards, "He needs help! He is showing signs! The broadcasts mention the symptoms--"
A Guard indifferently responds, "There are barely any medical supplies. The Orange Guard has commandeered them. WE need them more than some piece of fat lard who won't be missed."
"Then get him a bloody mask, you bastards! Can't you at least spare that?! Have you no decency, no consideration for the sufferings of--of your fellow man?!"
"Easy for you to talk, treasurer. We're in this mess partly thanks to you lot."
"Be that as--as it may, this innocent man has NOTHING to do with any of that! Get him a mask or--or even a simple cloth with which he can cover his mouth and nose, at least!"
The man's coughing ceases. He finally speaks, his voice hoarse and low, "He is...please, help me. I feel...horrible. Need treatment."
Thomas puts his hand on the tramp's back and sets him upright, "What's your name, friend?"
"Hugo. I was in--in one of our Frontier forts. I am an accountant by trade."
Hugo has a few pimples all over his round face. His black hair is matted and sticking out all over the place. He coughs a few more times, each time a bit harder than the last.
He goes on to explain, "We had no civilized form of communication at the Frontier fort. It was only when I was on--on a cruise ship bound for the Core when I heard the first news broadcasts via the ship radio. Panic set in almost immediately. We couldn't risk infection...I took a lifeboat when the survivors were--were asleep."
Thomas inquiries, "What happened to them?"
"I don't know. All I knew is that I had to get out of there. I know not how it's possible for a ship that far out at sea to become infected, but we were. People were dropping dead like flies. Someone had already thrown the bloody guns overboard...I couldn't risk being shot if I was caught...or worse."
"How long ago was that?"
"Weeks, I think. I only arrived on--on your town's shores a day ago. I hid out in a shack and slept it off. Until these bastards found me and started asking all sorts of questions."
"Like?"
Hugo glowers at the Guards, who now have their backs turned, "They wanted money - a bribe. Can you believe that? Our venerable Orange Guard stopping to such low levels unbecoming of their capes, rifles, and cloaks."
Thomas mulls a few things over in his mind, "I--I see. I can't say that I'm surprised, honestly. Loathe as we are to admit it, even the Orange Guard have families and loved ones to--to worry about. I don't blame them, I really don't. They are simply doing their duties and carrying out the orders of the Lord Emperor Himself."
Hugo weakly asks, "Do--do you know what's going to happen to me? I feel like shit, like absolute shit. I have trouble breathing. Even with my spectacles - which are no longer on my person, I'm afraid - I had trouble seeing. I had to squint till I developed a headache, and my ears are ringing constantly."
Thomas fixes the other man with a look of sympathy. His shoulders sag and he sighs, "I am...sorry, but it's probably too late for you."
Hugo's expression falls. He weakly asks, "How can you be so sure?"
"Trust me, I know. When you've been staring death in the face for as long as I have, you come to develop a sort of instinct. Your body betrays you, my friend."
Hugo buries his face in his burly hands and weeps. His muffled sobs fill the cell.
Thomas pats the other man's back and tries to encourage him, "Like me, you might survive. Maybe the Emperor looks favorably upon you? Maybe you are in the good graces of who you need to be. Have faith in--in Emperor, my friend..." he trails off, not even sure if he himself has any faith left.
He then queries, "What is the situation on the Frontiers?" This is more of an attempt to divert Hugo's attention towards something else, rather than out of any genuine interest.
"Catastrophic," Hugo responds, further words failing to come out.
The desperate man asks, "Is--is there no cure for my predicament? No tinctures, tonics, or remedies." There is but a hint of hopeful anticipation in his voice.
Thomas smashes the last vestiges of hope like a boulder by saying, "No. None that any of us know of, f--friend. Guard! Please! This man needs medical attention! He needs--"
The Guard retorts, "The infirmaries are already full! What the hell do you expect me to do, treasurer?! March in and shoot dying people so this washed-up tramp can have his turn?!" He storms out without bothering to hear what the former treasurer has to say.
Hugo gathers up some hay and lays his weary head on it. He shuts his eyes. Despite the hacking coughs, he soon dozes off.
"I'm sorry, my friend," Thomas whispers, "I'm so, so sorry." Even he succumbs to sleep. His body has already been taken to its limit - and beyond. His senses are dulling. Yet, through some cruel twist of fate, he is still here.
Nightmares and rather inexplicable visions torment both men. Hugo's eyelids flutter wildly, as he tosses and turns in his sleep. Thomas groans and begins rambling incoherently.
Hugo's head lolls back and forth.
The toll of the Abbey's bell serves a rather rude awakening for the pair. Thomas's eyes snap open, and he rises with a start. Hugo lazily props himself up by his elbows and yawns loudly.
A Guard peers through the barred window of their makeshift cell and says, "Morning. You're alive. Hm. Wonderful. Wait here. I'll be back with your meals." The contempt in his voice is barely hidden.
Thomas calls out, "What's the situation? Have there been any developments?? Guard! Guard! Have the cases gone up or down! BLAST!" Spittle flies out of his mouth.
The Guard doesn't deign to reply and instead departs, his heavy boots ringing out in the distance.
Hugo sits up and looks around. He rubs the side of his head and groans as he pops his stiff joints. He looks at Thomas and asks, "What was it that you asked? Cases?"
"Hmph. Yes. A very good morning to you, too."
"Of course. Manners cease to mean much in such dire times as these, I suppose. Good morning. How do you do?"
"Hmm. All is as it should be, I suppose. It is increasingly harder to come by proper information these days, Hugo. The radio broadcasts are sparse and the information that the IMOH puts out is - in my opinion, at least - rather inconsistent and all over the place. Even the Orange Guard is becoming unruly."
Hugo rubs his grumbling belly, "I'm hungry. I could go for some wild hog right about now."
Thomas bitterly chuckles, "Wild hog, eh? Even the animals aren't immune from this scourge, my good friend. Not even the produce of our great lands is safe. In the early months, they destroyed tons of foodstuffs. I only saw the Orange Guard carting it all away - I couldn't tell you for certain if they were contaminated or not, if you get what I'm saying."
"Emperor 'ave mercy. Even the livestock have started showing symptoms?"
"Oh, yes. They either have to be put down or consumed quickly - but only after cooking them for hours on end. Even that's no guarantee that they're safe for...err...consumption."
"Well, it's a damn shame. What kind of slop is on the menu, then?"
Thomas dusts his hands and pats his cheeks a few times, "You'd be lucky if the gruel isn't thin. My advice? Just be grateful and--and swallow what you're given, eh? Now's hardly the time to complain, is it? Especially when people writhe and suffer in our infirmaries. Or what's left of them."
A Guard appears with a tray. On it are two bowls of disgusting-looking, watered-down gruel. Steam barely rises from the bowls. Thomas and Hugo share a look with each other and sigh heavily.
The Guard slides the tray through an open slot, "That's all you'll get."
Thomas tries his luck, "Guard? What is your name?"
The member of the Orange Guard brusquely answers, "Edward. What's it to you?"
"You're new here?"
"Transferred from up north. Regiment's been taking heavy losses lately. High Command is giving the troops the ol' shuffle around to keep them on their toes. I've seen my share of action. Not just here, but in the Great War, too."
Hugo chimes in, "Thank you for your service, Edward."
Thomas fervently nods, "Yes, yes. Very much so. Edward, would you be a--a lad and perhaps fetch us some bread? Even if it's a bit moldy, I--we wouldn't mind the extra bit of nutrition."
"No."
Thomas tries to be nicer, "Come now, please? How would you feel if you were in our position? We're not as young and spry as you are. Even though I cannot see your face, I am sure that you are still quite young in years. Younger than myself, at the very least."
"What makes you lot special?"
Thomas shrugs, "N--nothing, really. It's just that my friend and I would--do need our strength if we are to, perhaps, contribute in some way or another. We still have our uses, my good sir!"
Edward derisively asks, "Then why are you both in here?"
Thomas blinks a few times before replying, "Err...circumstances beyond our control."
The member of the Orange Guard sharply exhales, "You want some bread, eh?"
Hugo has a glint of hope in his droopy eyes, "It would be nice..."
Edward pulls the tray towards himself, lowers his mask, and spits in the two bowls of gruel. He brings the mask back up to his face, turns on his heel, and calmly strides away.
Hugo's face twists into a horrified grimace.
Thomas's expression falls. When Edward pulled down his mask, he caught a glimpse of a few pockmarks not too dissimilar to his own. His eyes slowly trail down to his hands, which are also a tad scarred.
Thomas hopelessly mumbles to himself, "We--we are citizens of Empire. We have rights, privileges. We aren't savages or--or second-class citizens, are we? How could this happen...we are the very subjects of His Holy Highness."
Hugo drags the tray closer and grunts, "This Empire has gone to shit. I'll eat pigfeed if I must, but I simply have to put something into my belly."
Thomas vacantly stares ahead, "Eat the other one and be merry, my friend. I have...lost my appetite. Losing my sense of taste was--was already bad enough. Why would a member of the venerable Orange Guard do such a thing?"
Hugo is already chugging down the second bowl. He says between slurps, "Who knows? Power? Desperation? Someone weaker to take out one's endless frustrations on?"
He smacks his lips and nods approvingly, "Hmm, didn't taste half as bad as it looked. And it was also a bit sweet."
Thomas dryly points out, "Nowadays, they resort to putting sugar in everything - gives us more energy for the...days ahead. The grim days ahead."
"I see. Any idea when we will be let out of this prison? Does this place have a tavern or--or a pub?"
"You can forget such things. The local tavern is essentially what the scientific minds - or those still alive, anyway - are calling the focal point or ground zero. All manner of people congregated there. And all manner of people perished there, too. The only reason that it hasn't been burned down is due to certain risks associated with burning down Imperial heritage sites and such."
The days turn into weeks. Hugo dies an agonizing death on the floor of a hospital pushed far beyond its limits. His corpse is pale and somewhat bloated. Thomas catches it again and this time, Lady Luck doesn't seem to be on his side.
Two pale corpses are wrapped in dirty rags and loaded onto a horse cart that is already weighed down by the multitudes of humans. The cart is pulled by a team of horses and the wheels squeak and screech.
The rider relentlessly whips the animals. He wears a tattered cloth around his lower face. His eyes are covered with a pair of goggles, and his ears are covered in a scarf given to him by his mother. He is only nineteen. He was promised medical care should he aid in the ferrying of the corpses.
The odors alone overpower him with a great sense of nausea. Regardless, he powers through it and ensures that the bodies reach their intended destination. It's on the outskirts on this seaside town, far away from where he lives.
He does not know how many people perished or are currently awaiting their turn. A few members of the Guard accompany the young lad, lest any brigands or other desperate sorts get any fanciful ideas.
The bells of the Abbey toll, its metallic sound echoing throughout the empty streets. It fades away as the horse-drawn cart leaves.
At an unspecified site, the bodies are unceremoniously unloaded. Members of the Orange Guard stand ready with their flamethrowers. Their tanks are full and their resolves are steady.
Their eyes are filled with a mixture of fear and determination.
Thomas's limp body is carelessly tossed into the pile. Hugo's body goes into a separate pile, as is mandated by the new rules and regulations.
On the loud command, the flamethrowers are raised high in the air.
"READY!" a feminine voice bellows.
She goes on to declare at the top of her lungs, "AIM!"
The Guards take careful aim.
"FIRE!" she loudly orders.
With their weapons pointed at the piles, they squeeze off volley after volley. A wall of flames spurts out of the nozzles and envelops the corpses. The fire rapidly grows taller and taller. Thick, acrid smoke plumes permeate the air.
One of the Orange Guard's heads twitches. Once it all dies down, they take their leave, turning their backs on the piles and the ashes.
On the way back, the members of the Orange Guard make a mental note to keep a cautious distance from any corpses that they might come across - even in death, one is still infectious.
Grim are the days ahead.
