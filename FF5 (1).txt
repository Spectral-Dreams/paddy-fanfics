Chapter One: Lena Adams
Where?
Magnolia City, USA.
When?
201X.
Who?
She walks down a busy sidewalk. It's a bright, sunny day, the sky a bright shade of blue. She's dressed in a beige duffle coat, slim-fitting slacks and black boots. A pair of inexpensive sunglasses cover her eyes.
Before heading for work, she always stops by the coffee shop off West Street. The bell rings as she pushes the door open. The hubbub of conversation is the first thing that hits her, and she lets her eyes adjust to the interior.
There's a young, blonde barista behind the counter, wearing a tight-fitting black top with a vague, cartoony image of a skull printed on it. The skull is winking. The barista's feet are already aching.
The frantic and chaotic bustle of the coffee shop doesn't let up. Inner-city youth are oft wont to frequent this "hip" establishment. The other minimum wage baristas are baggy-eyed and gaunt- they idly shamble back and forth, prepping coffee for demanding customers.
She takes off her sunglasses, revealing a pair of appealing amber eyes. She waits in line and has a guarded expression on her face.
A young man with spiked hair and an over-sized shirt orders a large espresso.
When the line thins, she finally reaches the counter. The barista smiles, revealing a pair of sparkling braces. His smile is as though a puppeteer has forced his cheekbones to move upwards.
"Hello, what can I get for you?" he asks, trying not to sound so dull and monotonous.
"Can I have a small Americano?" she asks, sparing him a look of sympathy.
"Sure. That'll be uh..." the barista trails off while typing up the order into the cash register. He stares at the glowing screen blankly.
She produces her card; the barista swipes it and hands the machine for her to enter her PIN. She does so without any fuss. After that, she produces some kind of scratch card from her purse.
The barista has a knowing look on his gaunt face. He snatches it from her, clicks a pen and fills in the fifteenth circle. When he's done, he hands it back to her.
He dryly says, "Your twenty-fifth will be free. Keep at it, Miss Adams."
She nods back at him and patiently waits for her coffee, taking a quiet spot in the corner of the shop. She clasps her hands together and waits. Her burgundy fingernails gleam as they reflect the soft interior light.
The barista makes her drink, writes her full name on the styrofoam cup with a sharpie, and calls out, LENA ADAMS! Got a small Americano for Lena Adams."
Lena gets up, grabs the coffee, thanks the employees for their hospitality, and leaves. The bell rings as she pushes the door open. Her jet-black hair is tied up into a high ponytail held in place by a red scrunchie.
She walks down the busy sidewalk while sipping on her usual morning beverage. Faint traces of brick-red lipstick stain the rim of the styrofoam cup.
Smatterings of wispy clouds float by in the blue sky. The sidewalk is crowded with the usual assortment of office drones, tourists, students of all types, and hipsters.
In more ways than one, this Northwestern metropolis is a pretty diverse, vibrant, and interesting place!
Lena turns and takes a left. She walks for half a block until she comes across a set of steps leading down to an underground train station. Namely, Crystal Street Station.
Crystal Street Station is nothing short of an architectural marvel- much like many other structures in Magnolia City. High ceilings, polished marble, and a series of (mostly) working escalators. And it doesn't hurt that the trains...almost always (barring certain exceptional exceptions) arrive on time.
Lena deftly weaves through the throng of people, trying to keep her footing. Not that there are so many people currently present, but the morning rush hour tends, more often than not, to be quite chaotic.
Miss Adams steps onto the escalator, and it takes her down to the subway platform. She waits for the train, standing idly at the platform. She pulls out her makeup compact, flicks it open, and gives her face a quick once-over before tucking it away back into her purse.
She is an Asian American woman in her late twenties. Her complexion is warm. Her pert nose has a faint splash of blackheads across the bridge- something that has come to bother her since quite recently.
Her angular features are enhanced by the sunglasses that she's wearing.
Lena cranes her neck and always finds something new to marvel at in Crystal Street Station. Numerous little details that, upon a closer inspection, have a tendency to captivate her attention for a moment or two.
Or more.
The announcer on the speakers goes on about a train arriving on platform two. Lena's on platform five, so it doesn't really concern her. A minute later, his smooth voice bears some bad news.
"Pink line will be delayed owing to emergency rail works. Nothing too major, folks. We sincerely apologize for the inconvenience."
Lena huffs a sigh, "Ah, great. That's just perfect."
She walks over to the nearby map, scanning over it with narrowed eyes. While she's not late for work, she doesn't quite like showing up mere minutes before the workday officially begins- something (for better or for worse) that she inherited from her father, Yoshiro Akira.
Lena runs her finger across the map and thinks about her ailing father, wistfully wondering if she could do anything for him. Her amber eyes become unfocused, staring off into the farthest distance.
She is snapped out of her thoughts when her smartphone starts to ring. Her purse vibrates in her hand. She fishes it out and sees that it's her mother- Susie Adams- calling.
She taps the green button and raises the phone to her ear, "Mom. H--hi. Everything OK?" Lena's voice is a little deeper than usual, but there is still a certain appeal to it.
"Hello, darling," her mother's voice is a lot more relaxed and melodic. Very Midwestern, very much dripping with genuine honey and not the vague and self-absorbed fakery that so many other people tend to exhibit.
"Yeah, I'm fine. How's dad?"
Susie chuckles feebly, "Oh, you know how Yoshi gets."
"Yoshi", the cutesy nickname that she has for her husband.
Lena smiles a little and asks again, "No, but really, how is he?"
"Listen, you don't worry about the two of us, OK? We're more than capable of taking care of each other. Live your life in Magnolia City. Work hard, have fun, meet somebody special, the usual! And listen, hon, about the--"
Lena emphatically cuts her mom off, "No! I insist, OK?! I saw the horror show that is dad's medical bills! I AM sending you some money and you're accepting it, no arguments!"
Susie's sigh of resignation says it all, "Are--are you sure, hon? What about YOUR living expenses?"
"I'm fine, I'm managing. Somehow, I'm getting by."
"I'd holler for your dad to come say hi, but he's resting his legs, eyes, and everything on his usual spot."
"The chair on the porch outside?"
"Mm-hmm. Sometimes, I--I--check his pulse to make sure that...y'know?"
Lena tightly winces, "Mom..."
Susie's voice breaks and she sniffles, "Don't you worry about us, Lena. We--we're getting by just fine without you here. Hey, I can hear the choo-choo train in the background. Wait, aren't ya usually on it by now?"
Lena softly chuckles and looks over her shoulder, "Delays. Nothing outta the ordinary. Give dad my love, OK?"
"Alright, hon. Have a great day at work!"
"Yup, that's the plan, heh."
Susie's tone of voice is stern, and she speaks in a slow, measured cadence, "Honey, promise me one thing."
Lena blinks and tilts her head to one side, "Sure, mom. Anything."
Susie breathes in, breathes out and lowers her voice, "Promise me that you'll be careful out there, OK? I...occasionally read some stuff on websites about Magnolia City. Now, don't get me wrong: it's a beautiful place- as I'm sure you're well aware, haha! But...there are rumors floating around. About what really goes on out here."
The train hisses to a stop on platform five and the doors slide open. Lena narrows her eyes and walks inside while asking, "What do you mean, mom?"
"Don't you...know?"
"No? What are these websites, anyway? God, I sincerely hope that you're not falling for bullshit conservative conspiracy theories now. Ugh, mom, c'mon."
Susie sighs, "C'mon, hon, cut me some slack here. Like I'd ever lay eyes on those 'news' sites! No, I--I've heard things, read things that sound...legit. They say that there are some really dangerous folks living in the city."
Lena sits down on an unoccupied seat. The interior of this train is bright, sleek and modern- a far cry from the old, run-down cars that were efficiently done away with and replaced many, many years ago.
Lena counters, "Well, I mean, it's not like Indiana doesn't have its fair share of quote-unquote 'dangerous folk'. C'mon, mom, what are you trying to say?"
"I don't want to scare you, but...just be careful. I--I know you have the side...thing and all that. But when you're out there, doing that, just...promise your mother that you'll be careful, OK?"
Lena places her ankle on her knee, "You know that I am. I'm not stupid. Besides, my side 'thing' is actually something that I'm kinda proud of."
"Alright, alright. Don't get too cocky, missy."
Lena chuckles, "I love you, mom. Both you and dad."
Susie smiles broadly, "And we love you, too. We love you for who you are, no matter what. Always remember that."
The call ends. Lena throws her head back and sharply exhales. The train slows down, coming to a stop at its next destination. From there, it's a fairly short walk to her place of work.
Lena swipes her pass card at the turnstile gates, which then proceed to loudly and mechanically click into place. She walks onto the street and looks both ways before crossing the road.
Architecturally speaking, Lena's place of work- not just her office space- was done by the same people who did the Ford Foundation Center For Social Justice. The concrete is covered with gray-pink granite. It rises high up into the sky.
The interior is nothing short of breathtaking. The atrium has a multi-tiered garden that contains an array of rare and exotic plants from all over the world. At the very top is an observation deck that's open to the public.
An elegant fountain made of stone is right in the middle of the vast lobby. Water falls from the tip of a large, abstract monolith-looking sculpture and down into a shallow pool filled with coins.
The glass elevators are always available, and they whisk visitors up to their floors of choice. Lena's heels click against the polished floor as she walks across the atrium.
The rays of the sun stream in from the atrium, casting bright, yellow squares against the dark wood. Numerous office spaces and cubicles are located at every floor of this structure.
This place looks so very similar to the Ford Foundation Center For Social Justice, but it serves an entirely different function. And in a port city like this, that can be pretty relevant.
Numerous shipping companies have their corporate offices located on the topmost floors, along with other maritime-adjacent firms, water-tourism ventures, aqua-preservation initiatives, and so on and so forth and what have you.
The glass elevator takes Lena up to the second floor where the offices of Pluto Enterprises are to be found. Contrary to its name, it has nothing to do with the cosmos. In other words, just the generic, run-of-the-mill company in the Northwestern United States.
They do...stuff. Needless to say, no kids are gonna be job-shadowing their parents, older siblings, guardians, or relatives if said adults work in Pluto Enterprises. But they do pay their employees...decently.
There is far more to the surface than meets the eye.
Lena steps out of the elevator and walks over to the office space, which is located on the end of a long, narrow, and carpeted hallway. The aircon hums and cools the area to an agreeable temperature.
Large plexiglass windows allow sunlight to pour in. Several potted plants are neatly positioned along the expanse of the corridor. Dome-shaped surveillance cameras are positioned in such a way that nothing evades (or is even capable of evading) their line of sight.
The interior is decorated with various abstract- but tasteful- pieces of artwork. The walls are almost a duller shade of Lena's coat.
Security, for some reason, is taken rather seriously. An armed security guard (not of the rent-a-cop variety) runs a security wand over Lena's body, making sure that she isn't carrying any suspicious objects.
She agreeably complies but can't help but occasionally ask herself why such a mundane company would go through such elaborate measures. Hell, the NDAs that she had to sign were enough to set off more than two dozen alarm bells in her head.
Once the security guard is satisfied that Lena is in fact not a threat to the company, he gives her a nod, a practiced smile, and ushers her inside. Her purse slides across the Xray scanner and then into a retractable tray.
Lena slings it back over her shoulder, nods at the guard, and walks over to the sliding door. It gently hisses open once the green light turns on, revealing a bustling workspace.
A decent number of people are seated at their respective desks. An equal amount of them go to and fro, carrying armfuls of printouts and papers, walking off to do last-minute checks, grab a non-alcoholic drink, or have a quick smoke break.
Lena takes off her coat, revealing an off-white dress shirt underneath. She hangs it up and saunters over to her desk. She sits down on the squeaky maroon chair, its wheels slowly beginning to rotate.
She grabs her mouse, taps a few buttons to wake up the computer, and logs into her account. Her password is routinely changed by the people in IT. She receives an auto-deleting text message wherein she is ordered to memorize her password for the next thirty days.
This time around, it's "Pandas^TwoEight%$" (without the quotes).
Her fingers glide over the keyboard as she carefully types in her password. The monitor displays the company's homepage- a plain, dull, boring website that, admittedly, works just as intended. Her desktop background is fairly plain and usually on rotate.
This time, when she tabs out of the homepage, she's greeted by a picture of a sun-soaked vineyard somewhere in Italy along with a little factoid explaining the significance of that photo.
It's eight thirty in the morning. Work usually begins at eight forty-five, with a five-minute leeway given in case of delays.
The coffee machine on the far wall whirrs to life, spitting out a dark, black cup of joe. Lena's ears perk up at the whirring sound of the coffee machine. She...has a thing for coffee.
She swivels her chair, gets up, and walks over to the coffee machine. She gives it a once-over and ensures that the machine is filled, untampered with, and working properly.
Lena shall be paying this machine a visit more than five times during the duration of her workday.
Her job at the office is...eh. It pays the rent, bills, food provisions, cleaning supplies, and just enough left over for the occasional want.
For whatever reason, all of the cabinets here are biometrically locked. The only access-point that exists for this is a fingerprint scanner. Lena's thumb puts some pressure on the pad, and she watches the doors pop open.
Lena's job description is something along the lines of office clerk and admin assistant. Not glam or fancy by any stretched-up stretch of the stretchy imaginations!
But, to Lena, the paycheck is what counts at the end of the day. Her labor does sorta count as "skilled", not like the distinction ever had any genuine meaning in the first place- just another justification for the bourgeoisie to arbitrarily categorize and exploit people.
And this is precisely the sort of workplace where exploitation is...silent and unspoken of. Lena had to sign a pledge to not join any unions (or even commit the dastardly thought crime of thinking about joining up with one), lest she's subjected to termination without severance pay.
Lena puts a stack of papers away in a sliding drawer, sighs, and grabs her first cuppa joe for the long day ahead. The workday officially ends at four forty-five in the afternoon- a good eight hours!
But the clock-watchers are already...eyeing the clocks with their baggy eyes. Like hawks, they refuse to blink as the big hand steadily ticks away. Ticktock, ticktock.
When the alarm on Lena's phone goes off, she lets out a soft yawn and stretches her arms out. She blinks many times and averts her gaze from the monitor, rubbing her eyes with her hands.
Lunchtime! That's why she set the alarm on her phone; she even set up a reminder on her computer. In case she's taking a call or forgets, the other helpful reminder will surely swoop in and save her from overworking and exerting herself.
Loathe as she might be to admit it, she does tend to push herself a bit too much.
There are plenty of small, cozy restaurants located on the ground floor, and Lena is on speaking terms with the majority of them. While she's not exactly a regular (sometimes, she brings lunch from home), she's known as a decent patron.
She walks past the joint that ONLY serves sushi. Despite being part Japanese herself, Lena can't stand the smell, texture, or feel of raw fish. And if she's on a date, she's just about to ready to walk the fuck out if the potential date drags her along to get Japanese food.
No hard feelings, but she's just not a fan. But her ethnicity has gotten her into one too many awkward and sticky situations than she cares to recount.
Lena goes for some pasta. Sautéed vegetables and two pieces of garlic bread accompany the main dish. She wraps her fork around the noodles, brings it up to her mouth, and begins to chew, slowly and carefully.
Nothing outta the ordinary, just a regular, boring meal that's sure to set her up for a satisfying evening- that's when the real fun begins.
She is, however, a bit concerned. What did her mother mean by "what really goes on" out here?
Lena has been living in Magnolia City ever since graduating from college. Hell, her first ever job was as an intern in the very same Pluto Enterprises; so, she's not only been with the company for quite a while, but a significant portion of her adult life has also been spent living in this city.
Lena asks for the check, pays the exact amount- nay a penny more- and leaves. She doesn't bother with tipping, believing it to be a useless gesture. A pointless attempt at papering over the pitiful pay of front-of-the-house restaurant staff.
Lena washes her hands, wipes them with the tissues, scrunches them up, and tosses them away in the appropriate garbage bin. She adjusts her hair, redoes her ponytail, and walks back out into the lobby.
Back upstairs, the guards nod and greet her, and she plainly returns the gesture without a smile on her face. She looks down at her phone and sees that there's still a few more hours to go till she's outta here.
The afternoon rolls by without any huge, fussy fusses being made. As soon as the sun sets, the sky takes on a purplish-orange color. It's just a little past five forty-five- girl shoulda been out of here an hour ago!
Lena groans, clicks her tongue, shuts off her computer, and trudges over to the coat rack. She slips into it with a practiced ease and heads out. She walks down the hallway, steps into the elevator, and taps the button for the lobby.
A cup of coffee warms her hands. The liquid is hot, sweet, and just the way she likes it. Lena likes her coffee in any way, just as long as it's coffee. Milk, no milk. Sugar, no sugar. Black, whatever. Americano, Espresso, this-cano, that-presso. Coffee is coffee and Lena is a sucker for it.
The elevators ding, and she steps out into the lobby.
Now, onto her side...gig. The hip kids these days call it a "side hustle".
Well, whatever the hell it's called, Lena's side thang is way more exciting than the dull and monotonous grind of her day job. She sits on a round, upholstered bench, folds her legs, and takes a moment to think things over.
And to get a bit of rest. This gig of hers is...demanding and kinda risky, too. But she happens to be quite good at it.
Once the coffee cup is drained, Lena stares into the emptiness therein and shakes her head, "Could use another cup." She tosses the cup away and sashays out of the lobby.
The sky above is slowly darkening. A warm breeze wafts throughout the busy streets of Magnolia City. People are everywhere, all of them headed to some place or the other.
Lena proceeds to walk along the usual route- her "office" is actually quite close to her main place of employment, so that's convenient for her. Not that she necessarily likes that, but it sorta saves up on the commute.
She walks at a brisk pace, keeping her head down and her hands shoved deep into her coat pockets.
The brownstone building isn't as tall as the place where Pluto Enterprises is located. Moreover, it's starting to show its age in certain aspects. There are many cracks along the lower portions of the exterior walls.
The air inside is musty, a tad muggy, and stinks of old-ass paint. But the rent required for Lena's office is on the...cheaper side of things. The elevator is out of order.
Lena rolls her eyes and trudges up the stairs. Her little, humble office is located on the fifth floor, and her feet take her there without any hesitation. The coffee that she's been drinking certainly helps!
The interior- much like the exterior- is also showing its age. Water damage runs across the walls, the carpet is frightfully discolored, and the lights are flickering.
Oddly enough, many amateur filmmakers come to this location to film their nineteen-forties noir films. The aesthetic is quite fitting, considering that this building hasn't really been seriously touched since then!
The text on the door simply reads: "LENA ADAMS | PRIVATE INVESTIGATOR" in black block letters and bold print. She's been meaning to get it changed when a person had some difficulty reading the letters, citing certain corneal issues.
Yep, she's a private investigator. A PI. A snoop. A sleuth. A gumshoe. But even after all these years, Lena is still an amateur at it.
But all that was about to change tonight. Soon and very soon, she shall be dragged into a world that she never even knew existed.
A world of secrets. A world...of darkness.
Lena opens the door and heads inside. Her cheeks puff as she exhales sharply and takes off her coat. She hangs it on the coat rack, removes the red scrunchie from her hair, tosses her hair around, and runs her fingers through her scalp.
She lets her hair hang loose and flips the light switch on. She blinks a couple of times and lets her eyes adjust.
A messy oak desk dominates a majority of this relatively tiny space. Rickety filing cabinets (sans fingerprint lock, heh-heh), stacks of overflowing boxes, a few wooden chairs, and fewer assorted potted plants are what the interior consists of.
Lena pours a half-empty bottle of water into the plants sitting by the windowsill. Their leaves look a bit yellowish, withered, and lackluster.
"Ah, shit. I haven't been taking care of them, haven't I? Oh, well..."
Lena shrugs and plops herself down onto the brown leather chair- a far cry from that squeaky maroon chair, that's for sure! She spins around a few times, kicks her feet up on the desk, and leans back, closing her eyes.
She flatly tells herself, "Any second now..."
Many seconds later...
The warm wind wafts in through the open window and causes the rainbow pride flag on her desk to flutter. Her ears do not pick up on any footsteps or anything of the like. The main office door doesn't even creak open.
Lena sighs, wiggles her toes, and slumps deeper into the chair.
She doesn't have any appointments today- this whole thing is very much a one-woman operation. She seeks the potential clientele out (either through word-of-mouth or advertising her services on websites like Facebook and Craigslist) and does her duties accordingly.
Lena gathers the info, writes up all the contracts, and sends the client on their merry way...or in tears when she has to confirm the worst by telling the client that their spouse is indeed having that affair.
She has been doing this for some time now, so this routine of hers has become somewhat predictable. The DSLR camera sitting on one of her filing cabinets is still powered up and ready to go, though.
Lena opens her eyes for a moment, briefly surveys the area, and shuts them back. She allows her muscles to relax, but not so much so that she's sleeping. Caffeine still surges through her veins.
And she'd never ever consider skipping her nightly cup of coffee.
A little over many seconds later...
Lena pokes her head out of her humble office and narrows her eyes. The other spaces rented out on this floor are occupied by varying small businesses that put the "small" in small business.
Since there's no place to fit a photocopy machine in her office, Lena has an arrangement with the dingy office a few paces away from her. Unlike hers, it has been done up with bead curtains and colorful, shiny things that catch the eye.
Mr. Troy Porter runs said office along with his wife of fifty years. Troy is an old African American man with frizzy gray hair and droopy eyes. He's slim and often dresses in khakis with the checkered shirt tucked in.
His business has something to do with the distribution and manufacturing of water purifiers- the industrial kind and not the fancy home and garden stuff.
Troy greets Lena, "Lena...here to chat or use my copy machine? Not that I don't mind, but you don't got no clients right now, so why not?" He turns his head down and peers at her through his thick-rimmed spectacles.
Lena shrugs, "Sure. How's your wife doing, Troy?"
Troy chuckles, "I'm lookin' forward to the next fiddy with that woman by my side."
While the printers at work are ten times fancier (laser!), Troy's aging inkjet printer will have to suffice. Moreover, Pluto Enterprises expressly forbids employees from utilizing company property for personal and/or recreational use. Even if it is an emergency, PE employees are prohibited from using company equipment.
Troy's office is also quite cozy and warm, which Lena doesn't mind.
He mixes a cup of tea with a bent spoon and asks, "So, how's the PI game these days?"
Lena dryly answers, "Still the same old, same old. Affairs take up almost all of my cabinet space."
"A damn shame, all that infidelity and such. 'Bout as bad as a malevolent foot fungus, I'd say."
Lena is not really one to pass moral judgment around like that. A job's a job and she gets paid (somewhat handsomely but not quite) to do what she does. The clients come in with their money and problems, the PI does her snooping, she gets paid, they usually leave (relieved or teary-eyed) and that's it.
Professionals are said to have standards, and Lena Adams is very much a professional. She also maintains some necessary distance (emotional or otherwise) from her work.
Troy goes on, "So, what was that you said when we first met? PI for the...?"
As if by reflex, Lena's lips form into a thin, barely noticeable smile, "A Private Investigator for the public good. That's...what I aspire to be. But I dunno, Troy. All I do is follow cheating spouses- alleged or otherwise- around with my DSLR, hide in bushes, and tail people. I don't do anything..."
Troy takes a sip of his tea and completes her thought, "More? Meaningful?"
"E--exactly!"
"Took the words outta your mouth, didn't I? Don't worry, you'll find something. One of these days, somebody's gonna come waltzing in through them doors and they're gonna need your help, girl. The question is..."
Lena leans closer and narrows her eyes a bit, "Hmm?"
Troy also leans closer, his wrinkled and weathered face contorting into a crooked grin, "Are you the right woman for the job? Will you be up to the task?"
Lena ponders over this, shrugs, and nods. Troy gives her an understanding smile and pats her on the back. He hobbles away to get another cup of tea for himself. He hollers out, "Anytime you need anything, just swing by. Hell, we practically neighbors."
Ms. Adams chuckles, shakes her head, and grabs the printouts from Troy's inkjet printer. Mostly legal stuff, licensing requirements, tax forms, and other such boring, dull paperwork that is all part and parcel of being a gumshoe in this city.
While perusing over these documents, Lena hears footsteps at the edge of the hallway. Her ears twitch as she lifts her head and sees a man in his early forties nervously standing outside.
He has a slightly bewildered expression on his face, his brow furrows, and he stammers, "Ah...Lena...Adams? PI?" The man speaks with a Southern twang that is hard to pinpoint.
"Yes, that would be me. And you are...?"
He takes off his red trucker cap, ruffles his greasy hair, and enters Lena's office. He sticks out his hand for Lena to shake. He's thin, wiry, and on the shorter side. His skin is pallid and there are really dark bags underneath his blue eyes.
Lena shakes his hand and motions for him to sit down. He does, placing his cap on his lap and rubbing the palms of his hands together. A nervous shiver runs down his spine as he looks around, his eyes scanning over the PI's small office.
The PI presents a calm, non-threatening, non-judgmental aura as she leans back into her own chair. She waits for him to make the necessary introduction and how he found out about her services.
The man stammers, "M--my name's Jake Woodward and I found out about you on uh...Craigslist."
"Ah. And how may I help you this evening, Mr. Woodward?"
"C--call me Jake, please. No need to be so formal. M--mind if I call you Lena?"
Lena shrugs, "Sure thing, whatever you're comfortable with. So, Jake, how may I help you this evening?"
Jake takes a deep breath; his hands incessantly tremble when he lets it out. He clasps them together and squeezes tightly. It is clear that this man is in a great deal of distress.
Lena side-eyes him, "Are you OK? Would you like me to get you a glass of water or coffee??"
Jake frantically shakes his head and politely declines. His pupils are dilated and his fingers twitch. He looks around her office one more time and averts his gaze. Lena patiently waits for him to continue speaking.
He gathers his composure and begins, "I'd like to hire you."
As a way of showing genuine interest, she leans forward and nods, "I'm all ears. What seems to be the problem, Jake?"
"My--my son, Tyler."
"OK? And is Tyler in some kind of trouble?"
Jake looks at Lena with his bloodshot eyes and says in a quivering voice, "No. He--he's dead."
Lena winces, "I'm sorry to hear that. How old was he?"
