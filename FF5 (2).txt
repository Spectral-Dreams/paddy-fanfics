Chapter Five: Falsehoods
The sun rises and the Kindred in this part of the wide world begin to fall to their day-slumbering. The Childer of the night will have to remain hidden until the next sunset. Their bodies enter a near-Torpid state as they lay motionless and silent, like corpses.
The daylight hours will pass 'em by.
Francesca goes as still as the grave. Her chest doesn't rise or fall. Her eyes are closed and a relatively serene expression plays across her pale face. A few strands of blonde hair hang across her cheeks.
The searing light of the sun is blocked out by curtains and thick drapes that cover every single window in her Haven.
Lena, a mere mortal (for now), wakes up with a start a few hours later. It's a brand new, bright, and sunny Tuesday morning. Her eyes dart around the room for a few moments before her heartbeat calms itself down.
That infinite drowning-not-drowning dream always sets her on edge. She should see a therapist (or trained expert) about it, but...well...work. And mental healthcare is NOT covered under Lena's insurance plan, so she'll have to fork out the money to pay for therapy sessions if she wants to pursue it.
Lena sheds her clothing and steps into the shower. Her jet-black hair is soaked within seconds. She uses plenty of soap to scrub away all the dirt, grime, and sweat. As usual, the hot water turns lukewarm by the time the gumshoe is done cleaning herself.
While she wraps the towel around her naked body and heads back into her bedroom, she tells herself, "Tuesdays. Not as bad as Mondays. Just work, work, work, work, and more work."
She snickers and adds, "And an equal amount of coffee to go along with that. Heh."
As for Tyler's case, the most logical thing to do is to expand the search- somebody (or something) is bound to have seen something, namely the car that hit the poor child and left him to die on a street in Magnolia City.
The PI will dutifully poke around. But she's also got other cases on her plate as well; some people are paying decent bucks for her services. It's mainly tailing spouses who may or may not be cheating on their partners. Or looking into whether or not their children are messing with drugs or boozing.
Or fooling around.
Miss Adams swings by GROUND BEANZ for a cuppa coffee and a quick bite to give her energy to face the long day ahead. From there, it's a fairly simple walk to Crystal Street Station.
The train came one minute late. Lena shakes her head and steps aboard it, lightly jostled by the movement of the vehicle as it departs the station.
While sitting on a relatively clean seat, Lena taps her foot incessantly while she waits. She pulls out her phone and Googles: "Thrown into ocean infinitely falling dream meaning."
The results are...decidedly mixed (and this was before Google's somewhat annoying AI overview thing!). Some say that it symbolizes something bad happening in the near future, while others state that it's about a person's fears or some such. A few claim that it corresponds to feeling overwhelmed or scared.
She taps on the first link on the first page and starts to read the text aloud under her breath, "'Perhaps you are struggling with an area of your life and are drowning in the turmoil surrounding said area.' Wow, real helpful."
As the train chugs along, she keeps reading this short article while taking sips of her piping hot coffee. It goes on to suggest that she should try to gain a better understanding of her fears and what's causing her to feel overwhelmed.
"Yeah, no shit. If only I could actually afford something like that..." she quietly mutters, still skimming through the text.
When the train stops at the intended destination, Lena sighs, stands up, and makes her way out of the train and onto the platform. The underground air is cool and damp, but it smells kinda stale.
She quickens her pace and is soon going up the glass elevators that lead to the Pluto Enterprises office on the second floor. She admires the interior of the building (and its amazing atrium) through the glass panes, smiling to herself all the while.
"You don't always get a view like this," she tells the bored-looking intern standing apart a few feet from her.
The college kid nods politely and wordlessly. His suit looks quite oversized, and he keeps readjusting his tie. The young man is obviously new on the job and is still trying to earn his place in the big wide world of...whatever it is he's doing.
Lena passes the security check and gets ready for work. She can feel the eyes of her co-workers burrowing into the back of her head as she logs into her terminal and sees if there are any new messages for her in her inbox.
After yesterday's eventful events, everyone in PE is a bit on edge today. Still, Lena puts on a brave front which convinces everybody that everything is normal, although her co-workers aren't wholly buying it.
Some give her a wide berth or outright avoid her as though she were oozing infectious gas from every pore of her skin. Many others, however, are more sympathetic and kinder, wishing her well and saying that they hope she'll have a great day at work today.
Lena goes about her day job. When it's time for lunch, she heads downstairs and walks past numerous kiosks, pop-up stands, and vending machines before settling on buying herself a chicken sandwich from a bakery.
It comes packaged in a transparent container. She sits by a fountain and begins to eat. Her amber eyes scan the area for any familiar faces, but none stand out in the crowd. A half-consumed cup of coffee is beside her, slowly going cold as the minutes pass by.
The sammich is adequate, if a little cold. She tosses the empty and crumb-filled container into the colored bin, stands up, dusts off her pants, and goes to the bathroom to freshen herself up a little. She checks her teeth and her makeup, then heads back up to the second floor.
As she's about to step inside, a call comes in on her phone. Lena sees the caller ID and answers it, "Toby?"
"Hi--hi. Are you free to talk right now?"
Lena leans against a wall and looks at her co-workers who are going in and out of the office, "Sure. What's going on?"
Toby nervously replies, "It's Eddie. I--I'm kinda worried about him."
Lena's brows knit together in a frown of concern, "Did something happen to him?"
Toby takes a deep breath, "Well...he's been...showing off a lot lately. I dunno where- or how- he gets all of this cash from. He won't tell me. I--I think he might be into some stuff, ya know? I don't wanna say or--or assume anything nasty or unpleasant, but I'm concerned for him. I mean, we're best friends and all, and I just wanna look out for him."
Lena sighs, "Have you tried speaking to him about it and asking how he suddenly got a ton of cash?"
"Oh, I have, yeah. He just dismisses me and brushes me off. Sound familiar?"
Lena clicks her tongue, "Seriously? Look, I have work to do and I really should be getting back to it, alright?"
Toby desperately asks, "What should I do? I have to work with him at the garage day in and day out, y'know? I dunno how much longer I can just pretend that nothing's wrong. Every time I look into his eyes, I get the impression that he's hiding something huge from me...from US!"
Lena rubs the base of her neck and dryly smiles at a passing co-worker, "Listen, I'm gonna try and see what I can do, alright? I'll speak to him. But I can't make any promises- I'm just too busy right now."
Toby stammers, "And p--please leave my name outta this, alright? I don't want him to know that I...that I told you, ya know?"
"I won't. Don't worry."
"Have a great rest of the afternoon, Lena!!"
"Hm. You too, Toby. You too. Bye," Lena ends the call and shakes her head in dismay. She blows a raspberry as the questions begin to swell up in her mind. They swirl around up in her noggin along with all the other queries, doubts, suspicions, and concerns.
She slowly makes her way back to her desk and settles into her chair. The afternoon sun bathes the city in a yellow-white glow. People hurry to their homes, streetcars move at a snail's pace, and trains come and go.
If Ms. Adams has time, then she'll see what can be done about Eddie, but nothing beyond that, of course. As far as the PI is concerned, the guy probably broke his piggy bank open or saved up and is now spending it all on...whatever.
But a part of her is, admittedly, quite curious to know where all the money is coming from and how Eddie has been managing to spend so much so quickly.
This is just another task piled on top of her (already) mountainous list of to-dos and chores, however, and Lena isn't even getting paid to figure out the mystery behind Eddie's sudden influx of cold, hard cash.
By four forty-seven, Lena sluggishly stands up from her chair, gathers her belongings, and bids a good day to everyone who isn't actively ignoring or avoiding her. Tight smiles and waves come her way as she struts past everyone and eventually steps outside and onto the streets of the city.
