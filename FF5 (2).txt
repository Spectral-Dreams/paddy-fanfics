Chapter Four: The Orpheus Ruse
Until then, life goes on as normal.
Lena returns home, slips outta her dress, changes into a clean set of work clothes, does her hair and makeup a bit, and finally heads off to work. The PI heads to Crystal Street Station and patiently waits for the next train to arrive.
As she stands there, she scrolls through her phone and looks at some random pictures in her gallery. She sighs and puts her phone away. The train arrives, and Lena gets on.
Most of the office-adjacent types stare blankly ahead or look down at their phones or tablets. One of them even cracks a weak smirk and gives the PI a lingering once-over. Lena doesn't even notice the gesture.
She sits in the corner and waits for the choo-choo train to take her to her destination. She gazes around the place and contemplatively mulls some things over. Her mind wanders over to a myriad of different subjects.
The not-so-distant past, the immediate present, and the not-so-certain future. Little did she know that her own fate was intertwined with people who are (still) strangers to her.
Lena does eventually get to her workplace, but it's a few minutes past eight forty-five. She picks up the pace and hurriedly gets through the reception area, the security checkpoints, and then into the elevator.
She quickens her walk and bounds towards her cubicle. Her co-workers give her a curious look and say nothing as she sits down and starts working.
Once she logs in, she is greeted by the picture of a rocky outcropping- that's today's desktop background. She shakes her head, exhales sharply, and gets her day underway. She does the same, old, boring stuff.
(Mondays do be like that).
The hours go by in a blur of paperwork, document processing, database sorting, and filing. It's pretty dull. Lena is snapped out of her reverie when a man's nasally voice reaches her ears, "Lena?"
She looks up and smiles politely at the bossman, Mr. Gary Stuttgart, "Hm, what?"
Gary is a pasty man in his late thirties with a balding head and a wiry build. He's wearing a gray pinstripe suit and a solid red tie, his usual attire. His rimless glasses are perched atop his nose, and he always looks like he's shining. His expression is oftentimes difficult to read.
He dryly points out, "You were late today..."
Lena rubs her face and acknowledges, "I was late today."
He tilts his head and clicks his tongue, "OK...that's not how this works, Ms. Adams."
Lena stifles a groan, "I'm actually busy here, Gary..."
Gary strokes his thick mustache and evenly tells her, "Mm-hmm. Let's not repeat that again, hmm? Believe it or not, but you're a very valued employee here at Pluto Enterprises."
Lena scoffs under her breath, "Sure feels like it. Actually, I wanted to talk with you about something."
Gary leans against a wall and placidly enquires, "And what would that be, Lena?"
"Just something that's been on my mind, that's all."
"I'll see when my schedule is clear. If it is, I'll slot you in. Most I can spare is seven to ten minutes."
Lena's eyebrows go up, "Wow. Sure, that's more than enough time. Thanks. Now, if you don't mind."
Gary fervently nods and wheedles outta her sight, "Oh, oh, of course. Don't wanna overstay my welcome. Haha!"
(He's not bad people, but it's clear that he unquestioningly carries out the orders of his corporate betters- just a few weeks ago, he had to let a few of the long-term PE talent go because "streamlining" and "efficiency" and a whole buncha other corporate buzzwords.)
Ironically enough, PE reported a rather favorable quarter in its earnings forecast the very next day. But that's neither here nor there, surely!
Lena sips on a small cup of coffee. While doing so, her brows knit together in a frown. A bunch of random documents have found themselves in her work email account- she surmises that one of the newbies must've misfiled them or some such.
"What the hell??" Lena guzzles down the rest of the coffee and sets the paper cup aside. She sits up straighter and starts the mind-numbingly monotonous task of trying to figure out who sent these over.
However, her facial expression shifts when her amber eyes skim through the documents. Her pulse begins to race, and her heart rate starts to accelerate.
Lena reads out what she's seeing, "'On The Beach'. 'Incident Learning Process'. 'OBEs'. 'PLEs'. Gauze? Gauze?! The fuck is all of this?? What am I--I even reading here?"
Lena takes a closer look at the sender of the files. Unknown sender. Now, this just got hella suspicious (and intriguing). And hella scary, to boot. She cranes her neck and sees her boss in a very important meeting of some kind.
Company guidelines clearly stipulate that when such suspicious events occur, the whole thing should be reported straight to Mr. Stuttgart himself. Not IT, not the head of IT, not to anyone else, not to the other department heads.
Straight to Mr. Stuttgart. But he's in a meeting. He's sitting back in his fancy leather chair, and his fingertips are steepled together, his head is bowed, and his brow is furrowed. Looks like an intense budget planning session.
Or a great timewaster. Dime a dozen of those types of meetings in such corporate environs. Lena herself has been subject to quite a few.
She grunts and shakes her head, trying her hardest not to think about it. She eventually comes to the conclusion that this is just some type of innocuous error. Could it be a benign prank? A dumb practical joke designed to...what, exactly?
Lena goes over whatever came her way again. She can make neither head nor tails of it. For the time being, she figures that the best thing she can do is leave it all be.
The more she reads this stuff, the more her face scrunches into a confused scowl. Lena eventually decides to stop reading the mysterious documents and focus instead on the immediate tasks at hand- the tasks that her salary is dependent upon completing.
The real-world activities she needs to do, not the hokey pokey, mysterious shit. But...but a small part cannot help but wonder just what all of this entails.
While lingering near the watercooler, Lena mutters to herself, "'Spectres'. Since when did this company have anything to do with...Spectres? And what the hell is--"
She is snapped out of her hazy thoughts by an unexpected hand clapping her shoulder.
Gary. He has an inscrutable look etched onto his otherwise immaculately groomed mug. Armed security guards flank his sides. Lena looks at them, then down at her attire.
Gary lowers his voice, "Let's not make a scene in the middle of the day, Lena. Just come with me nice and calm-like, OK? I'm sure that there's a logical explanation for--for all of this."
The guards lift their chins and intensely stare at the PI, who wobbles on the spot. Her eyebrows go up, and her eyes widen. The others stop what they're in the middle of doing and keenly observe the proceedings.
"Gary?! W-what the--what are you talking about??"
"Please, Lena. Don't make this any harder than it's already getting. Come with us and we'll get it all sorted out, OK?"
Lena gulps and accedes to his request, "OK. OK. Look, I dunno what's going on, but..."
Gary ushers her into his office and locks the door. The armed guards stand outside, their chests are puffed out, and their grim expressions unchanging.
The bossman presses a button on a remote and the frosted glass turns fully opaque. Oh, shit's gettin' real!
Lena looks around her boss's office- not her first time in here. He gets his own aircon, a mahogany desk, the biggest ergonomic chair that she has ever seen, a freaking Apple iMac, shit-tons of awards for everything from mentorship to leadership, and a buncha other knickknacks.
(And that's not even including the lucrative stock options and bonuses that Gary is entitled to! And he counts as middle class!)
Meanwhile, people like Lena are practically living hand-to-mouth.
Gary sits down with a heavy sigh, "It has recently come to my attention that you have been the recipient of some...confidential information. Some sensitive documents. Very, very sensitive."
Lena licks her lips and nervously nods, "I swear that--that I had absolutely nothing to do--"
Gary lifts a stern finger and continues, "Please, don't speak until I finish saying what I have to say. After I'm done, you can say your piece."
She lowers her head and glumly stares at the floor.
The bossman brings his fingers together in a steeple, rests his elbows on his desk, and regards her with a measured look, "Miss Adams. Lena. You had no business seeing those documents...but you did. Those weren't meant for you. You should've deleted them the moment you saw the header."
Lena throws her hands up and complains, "I was going to, but I--"
Gary tuts, "Ah, ah, ah. Let me finish. Please. I've already ordered IT to perform a full, remote wipe of your computer. I'm sorry, but we at Pluto Enterprises take safety and confidentiality of our data very, very seriously."
Lena stiffens. The words get caught in her throat.
When her boss senses her silent acquiescence, he goes on, "All you had to do was come to me, Lena. Instead, you went ahead and, for whatever reason, decided to open the email and peruse the contents. Why? To put it plainly, what you did was extremely irresponsible."
"But--but you were busy, Mr. Stuttgart," Lena softly murmurs.
Gary shakes his head, "That's hardly an excuse. If it's an emergency- which it absolutely was- then you'd have had my permission to break the door down, pull a fire alarm, or scream to get my attention. But you did nothing of the sort."
"I--I didn't know."
"Exactly. You didn't know. Now, here's the hard part--"
Lena gasps, "You're not gonna lay me off for THIS?!"
Gary frowns and visibly bristles, "No. Please, don't be so overdramatic. We're not going to fire you. Don't worry."
Lena breathes out an audible sigh of relief. The sweat glistening on her forehead says it all. She clears her throat and belatedly apologizes, "Oh, s--sorry."
(Lena Akira stays in her job because of the medical and dental insurance coverage that she receives from being employed by Pluto Enterprises. She's had it like that ever since she started interning with them- straight outta college. If she leaves, she loses ALL of it. Simple as.)
Such is the ugly reality of the capitalist system, which is quite akin to a toxic, abusive relationship. And as such, people like Lena have no other choice but to sell their labor power in exchange for a meager paycheck.
Many in this world have no choice because they NEED the dosh to pay the bills and finance their basic needs- food, shelter, clothing, transportation, sleep, stability, etcetera.
It doesn't have to be this way...
Gary shuffles some loose paperwork and plainly states, "It's fine. We have no intention of letting you go. You've been with us...for years. You're practically a member of the Pluto family. Besides, the costs of recruiting and training somebody to replace you would be...well, substantial."
Lena faintly smiles, "OK...I'm sorry for--for whatever I did."
Gary evenly responds, "No problem. Don't do it again. Not that we're expecting something like this to happen ever again. But in this line of work, you just never know. Even if you think you're taking precautions, it can always go wrong. There's a million-and-one ways for things to go sideways."
Lena's face falls, and she lowers her gaze to the floor.
The bossman queries, "What exactly did you see in those documents, anyway? I'm sorry, but I have to ask."
"Just a bunch of stuff that I didn't understand, Gary. I mean, I tried making sense of it, but..."
Gary interjects, "We wouldn't recommend trying to make sense out of that. As far as we're concerned, it's just a bunch of mumbo-jumbo."
Lena asks, "Was it some kind of a prank?"
Gray briefly chuckles, "Maybe." His tone goes from stern to mildly evasive.
Lena clearly picks up on his change of demeanor, too. She tries her luck anyway, "How about an explanation?"
Gary straightens his posture and vehemently tells her, "You don't need any other explanation. You're in the wrong here, not the company. And I don't have to remind you of the clauses in your contract- no discussing these matters with coworkers, family, loved ones, lovers, friends, or basically anyone other than the department heads and directors of this company."
Lena blows a raspberry, "Mm-hmm. I've been here long enough to know how things are done. I used to bring coffee on a cart to a room full of investors, remember those days?"
Gary smiles and nods, "Yep. You were just getting started with us. Your first job straight outta, what was it..."
Lena's smile fades, "Indiana State University."
Gary's smile also vanishes, and he starts to look a tad uncomfortable. He uses his thumb to scratch his mustache, "The years certainly went by. Look at you now. Look at how far you've come. Gone are those wheel carts and in came a bunch of new, important responsibilities. Lots of responsibility."
"Oh, sure. Yay me. Since I have your attention now..."
Gary leans closer and stares at her intently, "Yes?"
"What the hell is an OBE or a PLE? What 'Beach' are we On?"
"I'm not gonna dignify that with an answer...or any answer, for that matter. Don't ever bring it up again- that's my last warning to you, Lena. Certain...things are out there, but we'd strongly encourage you not to think about them. Mankind wasn't meant to know everything."
Lena is in the habit of not taking orders too well, even when she doesn't have a whole lot of choice in the matter. So, instead of meekly accepting her boss's caveat, she presses on, "Honestly, it didn't sound like gibberish to me. Something's going on here and as an employee of PE, I have a right to know what's really--"
Gary explodes, "Enough!! Not another word of this!"
He calms down, "Please, Lena. You have no idea what--what--never mind."
Lena narrows an eye, "Since I still have your attention, why don't we talk about a raise, huh? Hell, you yourself said that I'm family, right? And if I know anything about family, it's that they have each other's backs, don't they?"
Gary sucks some air in through his teeth. He clears his throat, looks to the side, and ponders something.
Lena doesn't take her amber eyes off of her boss. He feels the pressure of her gaze and writhes with discomfort.
The bossman concedes, "An increase in salary is something that--that I...uh...can't personally authorize. All final decisions are at the behest of--of the CEO, who reports directly to the Board. If you really wanna push for a bump in pay, I can refer the matter over to HR."
Lena dryly tells him, "You better get started on it, then. I've worked here for a significant portion of my adult life, Gary, and I've gotten, like, coupons and shitty-ass gifts and cards. Nothing substantial. And don't even get me started on last year's crappy mugs and t-shirts."
"Alright, alright. I'll see what I can do. But you better not use this as an attempt to blackmail or--or strong-arm us. This isn't how things should be done, and we're not going to reward bad behavior."
Lena frowns, "What bad behavior? It's not my damn fault that a bunch of word salad documents found their way into my inbox, is it?"
Gary lamely concedes, "I suppose that you're correct; it isn't entirely your fault, yes. But that doesn't necessarily give you the right to get yourself involved in stuff that's none of your concern. And it sure as hell doesn't give you a right to extort or threaten your employer."
Lena scoffs, "Jesus. I'm not threatening anybody. All I'm asking for is a raise- it's reasonable! I mean, have you seen the rising prices? As it is, way more than half of my paycheck goes straight into paying the rent. And the rent's already gone up, too! Which means that I'm gonna have to shell out more and more!"
"I am truly sorry to hear that. We do offer courses on financial management and fiscal responsibility. You--"
"Fuck that shit. I want a raise. Not a lecture, not platitudes, not one more gift card. Not an umpteenth coupon. Money. Raise. And if it helps you sleep better at night, then I've already forgotten about...about all of that strange stuff."
Gary sighs, "I'll try getting that raise thing done. Most I can do is put in a--a good word for you and let my own bosses decide if you do merit a raise or not. Don't get your hopes up, though. Times are tough all around."
Lena groans, "Oh, spare me the sob story. I'm not stupid, this company is raking it in like nobody's fucking business."
Gary merely answers, "Mm-hmm. I suppose that may be the case. Now, if you don't mind. You and I both have work to do. The day's far from over." He makes something resembling a shooing gesture at her.
Lena picks herself up, bids a curt farewell, and heads back to her chair. She plops down on it and is greeted by a string of notifications popping up in her email account.
Her work computer has already been remotely wiped. An extreme measure, all things considered. It takes her a few minutes to get everything set up again. She has to manually change some settings, re-check for Windows Updates, and once the whole thing is back in shape, her work resumes.
Lena Adams is left with even more questions than she started out with.
The answers might (or might not) make themselves known to her in due time.
During lunchtime, Lena munches on a chunky burrito and thinks about matters NOT related to Pluto Enterprises. Namely, her cases as a Private Investigator for the public good.
Oh, yeah. She's got cases (plural) to tackle. Tyler's murder isn't the only thing on her plate, and an investigation does not develop linearly. She's got a few potential cheating spouses to tail.
As for Tyler's case, she figures that she'll have to broaden her search a bit. There were no cameras in the immediate vicinity where Tyler was killed. But somebody farther away must've seen the car which ran him over.
Michael (her mentor) would often tell her, "When there's nothing in the immediate radius of the crime scene, you gotta expand your horizons. If there's nothing within two miles of the corpse, you better take a longer look- five miles away. And if you can't see anything within a hundred miles or so, then the only thing you can do is to ask the spirits if they saw anything."
She had no idea what that last part meant. Most likely an attempt at a joke, if anything.
But anyway, Lena reckons that she'll have to expand her search. There has to be a cam (whether from some dingy bodega, an ATM, a pawn shop, etc.) that should've seen the car speeding away. If she's lucky, then she'll be able to spot a dent, traces of blood, and most importantly...a license plate.
Lena tosses the burrito wrapper into a nearby trash can and languidly leans back in her chair. While the food digests, she shuts her eyes and briefly recalls last night's sensuous activities. A sultry smile adorns her face.
Remembering the passionate session makes her inner thighs twitchy.
Afternoon soon gives way to evening. Lena is now in her PI office. The sun begins its grandiose descent beneath the horizon. She goes over the files pertaining to Tyler, trying to see if there's something that she's still not seeing.
Maybe a clue or a crumb of some kind?
Elsewhere in this Domain...
Francesca Livroni of Clan Hecata stirs from her day-sleep. Flashes of traumatic past memories involving fire, shouting, and strong words invade her mind and cause her to violently shudder. Her skin crawls.
She pops awake. No breath emerges from her nostrils or mouth. Not that it was ever going to. And it hasn't for a long, long time.
The Whip to the Hecata Primogen resides in a simple duplex house in a quiet neighborhood where the houses are evenly spaced out. About two blocks from her own dwelling is a small grocery store selling everything under the sun.
Not that a vampire like her needs the edibles, but they have good deals on other stuff- cleaning solutions, lightbulbs, toothpaste, gardening tools, and the list goes on and on.
The soft brown carpeting runs up against her cold and bare feet when she slips out of her double bed. Francesca flings the curtains wide open and watches the moon behind the cottony clouds. It's an exceptionally bright, starry night.
The Necromancer has a long night ahead of her. But, like any other self-respecting Corpse with a shred of decency, she heads straight to the bathroom. Vampires might not have need for the toilet, owing to how changed their physiologies become after the Embrace, but there are other reasons why one would visit a restroom.
Francesca stares at her reflection in the mirror. The tiled walls look pristine. She analyzes her features- the deathly pale skin, the lifeless hazel eyes, the remnants of dark circles underneath them becoming quite visible again, the pinhole-sized pupils, and the messy blonde hair that's all over the place.
She shakes her head and gets ready to bathe. While she brushes her teeth, she enters her walk-in closet and swiftly selects an outfit. After a vigorous teeth-brushing, she spits the white toothpaste out and mulls over the possibilities.
Fran thinks about the past- a lot. She remembers how she and many of her fellow Graverobbers were brought over here to Magnolia City, USA all the way from Italy. This was around the time that Benito Amilcare Andrea Mussolini had seized power.
The Italian Hecata- the ones who hadn't gleefully aligned themselves with the fascist scum, anyway- saw the proverbial writing on the wall and decided that it was high time for them to relocate. Or, at the very least, escape to a safer place.
(Not an easy thing to do when one is a cold, walking corpse incapable of being out in the sun for even a second!)
He...brought them here to Magnolia City. But not necessarily out of the magnanimity of his shriveled heart.
While under the shower, Francesca shudders. Not just because of the water. Rather, the reason for her shudders are thoughts of...him. Her Clan's Elder, the man who had used his own immense wealth, power, and connections to bring a shipload of his fellow Hecata to safer shores.
Francesca holds her naked body tightly, a few tears of blood dropping down the side of her cheeks. They eventually intermingle with the shower water and get washed away. The many (mixed) thoughts swish, swirl, and ferment inside her mind.
His words were stern...as were the many lessons that he had taught her. Francesca looks at her left hand. Burn marks and other severe injuries from the past mar her dead flesh. It's so grotesque that the very sight of it makes her sick- even while knowing full that she can't really "get sick" anymore.
Francesca's left hand is still usable- all her fingers are present and twirl as intended- but the burns make her skin look as Repulsive as that of a Nosferatu's. The blemishes, cracks, and scars are a never-ending reminder of the importance of certain things.
Family being the most important of them all. At least for the Hecata Clan.
Francesca wraps a towel around her body and heads out. She wipes her face and lets the memory play through her mind. As ghastly and traumatic as it is, the eternal nature of her existence demands that she keeps an ironclad focus on her duties...to the Family above all else.
The Family-Stiff (Nickname for the Hecata Clan) rubs her left hand with her right one. She winces each time she feels the roughness and deformities of her scarred flesh. When the memories begin to subside a bit, she snaps back to reality and reminds herself that...he is no longer here.
(The hand never healed after what was done. He made sure of it.)
As a matter of fact, he has not been seen for decades on end. And yet, not a night goes by when the other Hecata (especially her Primogen) are not reminded of their Elder's many...lessons. Lessons in the importance of sticking together and not rocking the boat.
Lessons in the necessity of Family, of the Blood, and of the world beyond. A reminder, too. A reminder of the Promises.
Particularly, the Promise of 1528. And he always knew about the five-hundred-year time limit. Granted, 2028 is still a long way off, but he was already making certain preparations. Even back in the last century!
These nights, most Hecata (Francesca included) sorta wait around and go about their Unlives as usual. When 2028 comes, then they'll see. They will hopefully know what to do when that night emerges from the shadows. Until then, one step at a time.
Francesca puts on a silky red shirt with the topmost buttons undone- this reveals a small bit of her cleavage- Ooh La La! She wears an above-knee-length gray skirt and a pair of heels. For accessories, she slips her leather glove onto her left hand (reasons), puts on some hoop earrings, and wraps some shiny bracelets around her wrists.
As for makeup, she settles in for some delightfully seductive and dark eye shadow, a touch of mauve lipstick, and some rouge. She applies some concealer to, well, conceal the deathly paleness of her skin- can't be too careful these nights!
She grabs her (recently acquired) Gucci purse and dons a pair of sunglasses (for the cool factor and nothing else). Her blonde, bobbed hair is a bit all over the place, but she's managed to dry and style it into something that looks decent enough for tonight's occasions.
Francesca heads downstairs and enters her study. She leaves the door open and sets herself behind the ornate, cherry mahogany desk. She pulls out a stack of letters and decides to go through them again. As of late, she's had this pang to go over her old missives.
She cracks a tiny smile at some of the steamy (at least by last century's standards) love letters. While she goes through the plethora of correspondence, the doorbell rings.
Food's here!
Her dead eyes flit to another letter, this one anything but flowery. The name on it has been partially scratched out- even after all these decades, the vicious claw marks are still discernible. She couldn't have forgotten about him even if she wanted to.
"Giordano," she says with a slight growl. She doesn't get any angrier than she already is, but hearing the name itself is enough to make her feel a stinging pain. And not just in her left hand.
The memories of his hideousness are somewhat fresh. And yet, he brought them all over here and (perhaps) saved their Unlives from the fascists.
Even now, his words echo and reverberate throughout her mind, 'I could've left you for Il Duce, but I brought you here. My Blood. My Clan. Never forget, Francesca. Family above all else. Even the Sects. Even the Eternal Struggle."
Francesca shudders and gets up to get the door. The second ring of her bell sounded more impatient than the first. This time, a man with a mohawk and tons of piercings stands in front of her.
He looks to be in his twenties. His tattooed hands are clasped together, and an expectant look is written on his face. He looks like the kinda guy who likes...Pain.
Francesca sighs and lets him inside her Haven. He knows what this arrangement entails. Hell, she surmises that this guy probably LIVES for the kind of Pain that she's about to inflict on him.
He didn't bring anything with him. He IS the food for the nocturnal predator of the dark nights.

He taps the side of his neck with two fingers, "Well, go on. Do your thing. Unlike others, I actually live for this sorta shit. Not the pain, mind you. But the euphoric rush, the explosion of ecstatic sensation and overwhelming pleasure that I feel when you..."

But her Clan finds all these sorts of people (scene kids, so-called "freaks", those on the margins of society, etc.) and has some sort of revolving door system going on.
Except the mortals never know who, where, and what will be next in line. All they know is that the bites of these particular vampires hurt. And they really do fuckin' hurt!
